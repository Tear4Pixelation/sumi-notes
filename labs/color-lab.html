Seed

The only thing taken from a seed color. Its lightness and saturation are discarded — that is why no seed can be “bad”.

Character

This is the neon–to–ink knob. 0 puts every color at its most saturated possible point (garish). The Schuler palette you showed me sits about 0.10 below.
Fraction of the maximum chroma each hue can reach at its own lightness. Never an absolute number — 0.20 chroma is dull for magenta and impossible for cyan.
The legibility floor. Any color too pale to read gets pushed darker until it complies. 3:1 is the WCAG non-text threshold; 4.5:1 is body-text strict and costs a lot of chroma in yellow/green/teal.
Deterministic per-seed wobble off perfectly even spacing. Schuler’s triad is 108°/140°/112°, not 120/120/120 — the error is what stops it reading as a formula.

Paper

Drag below ~0.5 and the whole algorithm mirrors: ink walks up from the cusp and the palette goes pastel on its own.

Families — base / dark / highlighter

Reference: Günter Schuler, “modern” — measured

All three sit at 97–100% of the maximum chroma sRGB can produce at their own lightness, and all three sit below their hue’s cusp by a similar amount (−0.08 to −0.13). They share no lightness and no chroma. The only thing they have in common is a fact about the display gamut — which is exactly why the relationship is invisible.
